    How the model works

    Shape. The STL is sliced into 240 cross sections along its long axis. Each slice gives the body width, the side silhouette, and whether the center is hollow, which is how the launch bore is found.

    Center of pressure. The app finds the fins (how many, their span, root and tip chord) and uses the Barrowman equations, the same method model rocketeers use. If it can’t find fins, it falls back to the cardboard cutout method: the balance point of the side silhouette, which is more cautious.

    Mass and center of gravity. Estimated from the walls and infill your slicer would print. Enter a weighed mass once you have one.

    Launch. Tank air expands as it pushes on the bore area while the rocket slides up the tube. The push lasts for the bore depth or the tube length, whichever is shorter, so a deeper bore gives a longer push.

    Flight. Gravity plus air drag, stepped every millisecond. Rockets with negative stability tumble, which adds a lot of drag.

    Calibrate it. Measure real peak heights with a clinometer, then adjust the efficiency and drag coefficient until the model matches.
